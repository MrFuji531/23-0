What’s behind 23-0,
and how Full Side works.

A working independent adaptation with five-player Classic and the current 23-player AFL team size, with a reproducible player database and an openly explained scoring model.

What I could verify

The live 23-0 home page offers a playable Classic mode and a disabled Full Side button. The Full Side description explicitly promises a 22-player team. There is no release date visible. I cannot establish why it has not launched or how long the current implementation has been in place.

Classic is a five-player draft: DEF, MID, RUC, FWD and utility. It draws a club and a decade, gives limited club/era rerolls, and scores a season when the lineup is complete. The official rules explain that ratings use statistics and honours relative to era baselines, spanning the 1990s onward.

The browser bundle examined was build 7e88b97. Its delivered game implementation supports five picks. Full Side is a disabled mode card; I did not find a hidden playable 22-player route in this bundle. That says nothing about unpublished work on the author’s computer or server.

Their player database

The downloaded catalogue contains 3,391 records covering 2,210 distinct player IDs, four decades and 19 club labels. A record represents one player at one club in one decade, rather than an entire career. The payload declares a 30-game threshold.

Fields include games, average disposals, goals, marks, tackles, hitouts, kicks and handballs; some later-era records also contain contested possessions, clearances, inside 50s, rebounds and other measures. It includes Brownlow votes, All-Australian and captain flags, finals played/won, inferred position information and decade baselines.

The frontend groups records by club and era and orders the displayed candidates by games plus three times Brownlow votes (the initial data index also sorts by fantasy average). Its draw code favours unused eras and excludes already-selected clubs in Classic. It also tries to offer players suitable for open positions. That restriction cannot carry over unchanged to 22 picks: there are fewer distinct clubs than roster spots.

Unknown: the original collection pipeline, upstream provider, exact positional inference, honour aggregation rules, scoring weights and final win thresholds. The fields are compatible with a statistical aggregation pipeline, but that is an inference—not proof that the author used fitzRoy, AFL Tables or any other specific source. Exact replication of the hidden scoring formula is not supported by the evidence.

The database actually used here

Classic and Full Side use 3,391 player–club–era records adapted from the original public catalogue, downloaded on 1 October 2026. The snapshot preserves its player IDs, names, club and era membership, eligible positions, games, votes and displayed averages. Its declared 30-game threshold is metadata; the supplied catalogue itself is retained without applying an additional filter.

The browser loads the local game-players.json and does not call the original scoring server. The included scripts/build-reference-data.py converts the public JSON and calculates independent percentile ratings. Publicly visible position eligibility is used directly instead of the earlier statistical guesses.

The earlier independent AFL Tables dataset remains in players.json as a research alternative: 2,110 people, 3,233 records, 310,402 match-player rows through 26 September 2026. Its reproducible fitzRoy/DuckDB pipeline is retained in scripts/build-data.py; it is no longer the active gameplay catalogue.

Scoring: deliberately open

The game ranks each statistic against player–club records eligible for that role in the same decade. A ruck’s hitouts are compared with other eligible rucks, not the many midfielders and forwards who record no hitouts. Ties receive a midpoint percentile. This reduces some era differences, but is not a professionally validated cross-era model.

RoleStat weights
DEF40% rebounds, 25% one-percenters, 20% marks, 15% disposals
MID55% disposals, 25% clearances, 20% tackles
RUC75% hitouts, 15% disposals, 10% marks
FWD75% goals, 15% marks, 10% tackles
INTThe player’s highest eligible role rating

When a metric is missing, the other weights are proportionally renormalised. The rating is 5 + 90 × weighted percentile + experience bonus + Brownlow bonus. Experience contributes up to 3 points at 100 games in that club–era. Votes contribute up to 2 points at 0.65 votes per appearance. Ratings therefore span approximately 5–100.

The team rating is a weighted average: Classic gives each of its five picks equal weight, with Utility using the player’s best role rating. Full Side gives each of its 18 starting players weight 1 and each of five interchange players weight 0.65. The 23 fictional opposition ratings are equally spaced from 24 to 94. These are game-design difficulty settings, not measured real AFL opponents. Your side wins a match when its unrounded rating meets the threshold. The displayed match order is fixed and shuffled. There are no random match outcomes or real-club fixture claims.

Limitations: position eligibility follows the original public catalogue’s broad DEF/MID/RUC/FWD labels, whose inference method is unpublished; these do not verify specialist key-position, tall or small-player roles; defensive impact is poorly measured by the available box scores, particularly in older eras; advanced metrics can cover only part of a decade; 2020 shortened games are not individually time-normalised; averaging includes late-career and substitute appearances; peak seasons, All-Australian honours, captaincy, premiership contributions, chemistry and opposition adjustments are not modelled. Brownlow votes favour midfielders. These are reasons to treat the result as a game, not a definitive ranking of footballers.

Placement is restricted to the catalogue’s eligible roles. Utility and interchange accept any player. A drafted player may move to an eligible empty position; occupied positions cannot be overwritten or swapped.

Full Side: current AFL team structure

  • Six defenders, five midfielders, one ruck, six forwards and five interchange players: 23 in total. Ruck-rover and rover are midfield positions, not extra rucks. This follows the AFL’s five-player interchange rule from 2026; the reference website’s disabled card still describes 22.
  • One club respin and one era respin across the entire draft, matching Classic. A reroll with no alternative is not consumed.
  • Spin before each player. Draws favour unused eras and unused clubs. Full Side allows clubs to repeat once no unused playable club remains, since 23 players exceed the number of clubs. A player cannot recur.
  • Player search, genuine role filters, eligible empty-slot moves, and mobile position drawers. The final placement opens a projected-record count-up over 1.4 seconds, with cubic easing and a grade fade. Reduced-motion settings show the result immediately.
  • Device-local draft recovery, seeded challenge links, completed-lineup links and a 23-round result display.
  • Shared lineups are embedded in the URL and can be modified by their owner; this is casual play with no authoritative leaderboard or anti-cheat claim.

The current version is static and can run from any ordinary web host. No accounts, paid data provider, server database or live API dependency are required. Snapshot refreshes use the included Python script. A trusted competitive leaderboard would require server-side validation and durable storage; that is outside this version.

Scoring balance audit

The earlier formula compared role statistics with every player in an era, inflating specialist ratings. In 250 earlier full-side tests, taking the first legal names gave 19–23 wins in every draft; taking the highest available ratings gave 23 wins every time. The earlier formula therefore made elite records too easy.

The revised model uses role-and-era peers, and interchange takes only an eligible-role rating. The completed reproducible audit contains 250 drafts per strategy in each mode (2,000 total). Full Side random legal selections averaged 9.61 wins (range 6–14); first-listed legal names averaged 18.52 (16–21); highest-rating legal choices averaged 21.61 (21–22). Classic’s smaller sample of five players has greater variability. These checks assess game difficulty, not forecasting accuracy.

Each result displays the individual ratings, weighted team average, line averages and all 23 comparisons. A grade describes the projected record; no actual competition ladder or finals bracket is simulated. The original 23-0 server formula remains private and is not replicated.

Verification

The engine suite checks 100 Classic and 100 Full Side drafts, ruck-to-bench eligibility, fifth interchange placement, preservation of completed 22-player saves, a required spin for each pick, automatic completion, reproducible results, era coverage, one respin per axis, position eligibility, empty-slot moves, save restoration and duplicate-player prevention. It also verifies that every catalogue record has source provenance and finite ratings. Browser checks cover drafting, rearrangement, reload recovery, completing a season, sharing and responsive layout.

The original Classic was played through all five picks, both respins and the results screen. It was also inspected through its published instructions, page HTML, directly referenced JavaScript and public player data. The independent game engine was written from scratch. The interface was subsequently restyled to closely match 23-0 at the user’s request, including its displayed logo.
